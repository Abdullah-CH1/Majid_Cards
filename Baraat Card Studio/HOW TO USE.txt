WHAT THE CLIENT LIKED (Sample 1) AND HOW THE NEW SET ANSWERS IT
- Real painted art, not clip-art. The new cards use real paintings, engravings and photos:
  17th-century Mughal album borders, Ohara Koson woodblock lotus and peony prints, hand-tinted
  lotus plates, carved marble jali. All are public domain / free licence (details in assets\catalog.json).
- Calm cream paper, thin gold Mughal arch, gold line mandala: kept as the "family look" in most designs.
- Clear hierarchy: Bismillah > hosts > invitation > NAMES (the hero) > Baraat > date > venue.
- Correct groom's-side wording: "request the pleasure of your company at the Baraat of their beloved son
  Abdul Aleem with Laiba".  (Video-studio cards read "Abdul Aleem & Laiba"; they have no hosts line.)

THE 25 NEW DESIGNS, IN FIVE FAMILIES
 Ivory and lotus (closest to Sample 1)

   so elderly guests can read them on a phone. Old versions: _backup_2026-11-15_before_readability\)
  1 Noor Mehrab   - (was Noor Kanwal) the client's favourite look made bolder: ivory paper, strong gold cusped arch,
                    gold corner mandalas, a big gold half-mandala rising at the base. SAFEST PICK.
  2 Nazakat       - soft blush paper inside a band of gold eight-point jaali stars, names in tall gold capitals.
  3 Jharoka       - bold gold Mughal window opening onto a 19th-century painting of the Taj Mahal, navy lettering.
  4 Haala         - sage paper; a great gold halo with a crescent circles the names like a full moon.
  5 Shabnam       - torn-edge cotton card on sage, bold gold frame with Mughal stars at the corners. Type-led.
 Royal jewel tones (evening Baraat)
  6 Shamsa        - Shah Jahan's gold-and-lapis sun medallion on midnight blue, bold gold frame.
  7 Zumurrud      - emerald velvet, wide ivory arch panel edged in burnished gold. STRONG BARAAT PICK.
  8 Zardozi       - oxblood, beaded gold-thread border and one eight-point-star zardozi medallion, like a sherwani.
  9 Firoza        - turquoise between two illuminated margins of gold lattice and eight-point stars.
 10 Sang-e-Musa   - black marble, one tall gold doorway arch and crescent, bold ivory lettering.
 Mughal manuscript
 11 Muraqqa       - a Shah Jahan Album page: gold-leaf-flecked margins, lapis and gold rulings, ivory panel.

 14 Gul-e-Jafari  - Mughal marigold paintings either side of an ivory arch on Baraat red.
 15 Gulistan      - 1755 Lucknow flower painting all over, words in an ivory cartouche.
 Engraved and botanical

 18 Nilofar       - Koson woodblock lotus in a cusped arch window, text below.

 Marble and modern luxe
