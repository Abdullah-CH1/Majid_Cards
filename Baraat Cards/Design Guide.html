Baraat Invitation: Design Guide

Abdul Aleem with Laiba · hosted by Mr. & Mrs. Col Asim · 6 designs, all built from what the client liked in Sample 1

What the client liked in Sample 1, and what we kept

  • The Mughal arch: a cusped (scalloped) arch framing the text. Every design has one; it is the "family resemblance".
  • Bismillah in calligraphy at the top, in the classic long-stroke style.
  • Gold line-work mandalas and lotus flowers, the soft, painted, traditional feel.
  • Centred, formal layout: hosts, then the couple, then date and venue.

What we made better

  • Real hierarchy: the names are now the hero (big gold-foil script), everything else is calm and smaller. In Sample 1 the names, date and venue were all the same weight.
  • Baraat wording: "request the pleasure of your company at the Baraat of their beloved son …". This is correct for a card sent by the groom's family. "with" between the names is the traditional groom's-side form (the bride's side would write her name first).
  • A "BARAAT" label with flourishes, so guests can't mix it up with the Walima card.
  • Richer colours: a Baraat calls for deeper tones (maroon, emerald, crimson, midnight). We kept two light options for clients who love ivory.
  • Gold-foil effect on names and lines, plus paper or velvet texture, so it looks printed and premium rather than flat.
  • All artwork is drawn by us (no stock images), so it stays sharp at any print size and there's nothing to license.
Closest to Sample 1 · safe pick

1 · Noor Lotus

"Noor" means light. This is Sample 1 refined: ivory parchment, a double gold cusped arch with a finial, lotus plants rising from both corners, and a large lotus in front of a gold mandala.

Royal · most "Baraat"

2 · Shahi Maroon

"Shahi" means royal. Deep maroon velvet with a faint jaali (lattice) pattern, gold corner rosettes, two glowing Mughal lanterns, and a raised ivory arch panel with crescent and star above it. A gold lotus and mandala sit at the base.

Maroon #5A0F1DIvory panel #F8EEDBBright gold #D4AD55

Fonts: Ephesis (names) · Cormorant SC (hosts) · Cormorant Garamond · Cinzel

"Maroon and gold is the classic Baraat colour. The ivory arch keeps the Sample 1 feel, and the lanterns make it festive."

Architectural · Mughal palace

3 · Zumurrud Jharokha

"Zumurrud" means emerald; a "jharokha" is a palace balcony window. The card is a palace façade: a row of leaf-shaped battlements (kanguras) along the top, a tall onion arch in the centre, two side alcoves with lanterns, and jaali screens and plinth below.

Emerald #0E3F30Gold #E2C47ACream text #F1E5C6

Fonts: Pinyon Script (names) · Cormorant SC · Cormorant Garamond · Cinzel

"For something different from red: emerald and gold, like walking into Shalimar or Shahi Qila."

Trending · artistic

4 · Pichwai Night Lotus

Inspired by Pichwai painting, the traditional art of lotus ponds that is very fashionable on luxury invitations right now. A midnight-blue sky with gold stars, a soft moon glow behind the text, a gold arch, and a lotus pond of pink and white blooms on gold-rimmed leaves.

Modern luxury · minimal

Clean and modern: a small arched window filled with gold jaali (lattice), and a round monogram medallion (A / L) where the arch meets the text. It has a calendar-style date block, lots of white space, and an Urdu closing line: "آپ کی شرکت ہمارے لیے باعثِ مسرت ہوگی".

Festive · traditional shaadi

"Gul-e-Surkh" means red rose. Crimson with a faint gold paisley print, a marigold-and-rose toran (door garland) draped across the top with hanging phoolon ki larhi, a gold arch, and red roses at the base.

Ask the client before final

  • Date and time. Both samples show the same day (Thu 31 Dec 2026, 2:00 pm, Noor Mahal Marquee). Is that really the Baraat, and is the Walima on another day?
  • Do they want the bride's parents' names (e.g. "d/o Mr. & Mrs. …"), a Rukhsati time, or an RSVP number? These can be added easily.
  • "Abdul Aleem" (full name, as in Sample 2) or just "Aleem" (as in Sample 1)?
  • Urdu closing line: wanted on all designs or only some?
